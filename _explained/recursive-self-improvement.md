Recursive self-improvement is the idea that an AI could help build a better AI, which then helps build an even better one. Each round could go faster than the last.

Today, people build AI models. They design the training, run the tests and fix what goes wrong. AI already helps with parts of that work, for example by writing code. Recursive self-improvement is what could happen if AI does more and more of the research itself.

> **Think of it like this:** a factory that uses its own machines to build better machines. The better machines build the next ones faster. After a few rounds, the factory changes faster than the people running it can follow.

The word "recursive" means the process feeds into itself. Better AI makes better AI research, which makes better AI.

Nobody knows how fast this could go, or whether it will happen at all. Some see it as a way to speed up science. Others fear that AI could improve faster than humans can understand or control it.

That is why AI labs watch for it. Anthropic's safety rules, the Responsible Scaling Policy, set thresholds for AI that can do research on its own or sharply speed up AI progress. Reaching a threshold means stricter safety measures.

**Why it matters to you:** if AI starts to speed up its own progress, the tools you use could change much faster. The key question is whether people stay in charge of each step.
