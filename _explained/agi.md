AGI stands for artificial general intelligence. It usually means an AI that can do a wide range of tasks as well as a person can, not just one narrow task. That is the common core. Beyond it, people disagree.

There is no single agreed definition. Here are three examples.

OpenAI's charter defines it as "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work". This is a definition about work and money.

A group of researchers, several of them at Google DeepMind, wrote a paper in 2023 that starts from the lack of a shared meaning. They looked at existing definitions and proposed "levels of AGI". The levels depend on two things: how well a system performs, and how broad its skills are.

Dario Amodei, the head of Anthropic, writes that he finds the term imprecise, and that it has gathered a lot of science fiction and hype. He prefers to say "powerful AI".

> **Think of it like this:** the word works like "a big city". Everyone knows roughly what it means. But ask how many people it takes, and you will get different numbers depending on who answers.

This matters because the answer to "have we reached AGI?" depends on the definition you pick. A system can pass one definition and fail another. The word is also used outside science. It appears in company goals, in funding talks and in news headlines, where it can mean different things in each place.

Three questions help when you see the word. Who is using it? Which definition do they mean? And can the claim be tested, for example with a benchmark or a clear task?

**Why it matters to you:** headlines about AGI can sound like a clear finish line, but there is no finish line everyone agrees on. When you read that AGI is near, or has arrived, look for the definition first. Without one, the claim says very little.
