A GPU, or graphics processing unit, is a computer chip built to do many small calculations at once. It was first made to draw graphics for games. Every pixel on the screen needs its own math, and all of it has to happen in a fraction of a second.

The main chip in your computer, the CPU, works differently. It has a few strong cores that handle varied tasks one after another, quickly. A GPU has hundreds or thousands of simpler cores, and they all work in parallel.

> **Think of it like this:** a CPU is a few skilled chefs who can cook anything. A GPU is a thousand kitchen helpers who each peel one potato. For a menu with fifty dishes, you want the chefs. For a mountain of potatoes, you want the helpers.

AI is a mountain of potatoes. Training and running a model means multiplying huge tables of numbers, again and again. Each multiplication is simple, and most of them can happen at the same time. That is exactly the work a GPU is good at. Modern AI chips also add special parts that speed up these table multiplications even more.

That is why the companies that make these chips, above all Nvidia, sit at the center of the AI industry. Every new model, data center and AI service needs them, and there are rarely enough.

**Why it matters to you:** the price and supply of GPUs set much of what AI costs. So news about GPUs is also news about how fast and cheap your AI can get.
