A data center is a building full of computers. The computers are called servers. They store data and run programs, so that apps, websites and chatbots work when you use them. The International Energy Agency (IEA) describes data centers as facilities that house servers, storage systems and network equipment, plus the equipment that keeps them running.

> **Think of it like this:** a data center is a factory where the product is computing. Power goes in, answers come out, and a lot of heat is left over.

AI needs more of them because AI models run on special chips that do huge amounts of math. These chips use more power than ordinary servers. The IEA says a typical AI data center uses about as much power as 100,000 homes. The largest ones now being built will use about 20 times that. The IEA also says data centers used about 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024, and it expects that to more than double by 2030.

Heat is the next problem. Chips give off heat when they work, so data centers must be cooled with air, water, special liquids or a mix. Cooling is a big cost. The IEA says it takes about 7% of the electricity in efficient large data centers, and over 30% in less efficient ones. Servers take around 60%.

Water is a trade-off. Google says that water-cooled data centers use about 10% less energy than many air-cooled ones. The price is that they use water. The right choice depends on the place, and Google says there is no one-size-fits-all solution.

The heat can be reused. In Odense, Denmark, a Meta data center sends its waste heat through large heat pumps to the city's district heating network. The engineering firm Ramboll says the plant delivers about 45 megawatts and was meant to supply more than 12,000 homes.

**Why it matters to you:** data centers decide how fast AI can grow, and they compete with towns for power, land and water. When a news story says an AI company is building a new campus, this is what it is building. It is also why your electricity grid and your local planning office may soon care about AI.
