A zero-day is a weakness in software or hardware that the people who made it do not know about yet. The US standards body NIST defines a zero-day attack as one that "exploits a previously unknown" weakness.

> **Think of it like this:** A zero-day is a broken window at the back of a shop that the owner has never noticed. A burglar who finds it first can get in, and no lock on the front door helps.

Many attacks use known holes. Those already have a fix, and the attack only works on systems that have not installed it. That is why updating your phone and computer protects you from many of them.

A zero-day is different, because no fix exists yet. Once the makers learn about the hole, they can write a patch. From then on it is a known hole, and the race is about who installs the patch in time.

Finding zero-days used to take skilled people a lot of time. That is why they are valuable, both to security researchers who report them and to attackers who use them. AI tools now help with the search on both sides.

**Why it matters to you:** You cannot patch a zero-day yourself. What helps is exposing less. Keep fewer services open to the internet, put logins in front of the rest, and update quickly once a fix arrives.
