The EU AI Act is the European Union's law for artificial intelligence. It works by risk. The more an AI system can harm people, the stricter the rules. The European Commission names four levels.

Unacceptable risk is banned. This covers AI that is a clear threat to people's safety or rights, such as social scoring. High risk covers uses that can seriously affect health, safety or rights, such as CV-sorting software or credit scoring that can deny a loan. Transparency risk means people must be told when they deal with AI. A chatbot must say that it is one, and deepfakes must be labelled. Minimal risk has no new rules. Spam filters and AI in video games are examples.

> **Think of it like this:** it works like food safety rules. A bakery and a factory that makes baby formula do not face the same checks. The more harm a product can do, the stricter the rules.

The law also has rules for general-purpose AI models, which are the big models behind chatbots. Their makers must meet duties on transparency and copyright, and on managing risks for the most powerful models.

The dates are the part people mix up. As of 26 November 2026, the Commission's pages say this:

**1 August 2024:** the law enters into force.

**2 February 2025:** the bans and AI literacy duties apply.

**2 August 2025:** the rules for general-purpose AI models apply.

**2 August 2026:** most of the law applies and enforcement begins, including the transparency rules. Some AI systems already on the market get a transition period for certain labelling duties.

**2 December 2026:** a new ban applies on AI that makes non-consensual sexual deepfakes or child abuse material.

**2 December 2027:** the high-risk rules for sensitive areas such as hiring and education apply.

**2 August 2028:** the high-risk rules for AI built into regulated products apply.

The last two dates were extended by a change law, the Digital Omnibus on AI. Dates have moved before, so check the Commission's page before you rely on one.

**Why it matters to you:** if you live in the EU, a chatbot should tell you that it is one. AI-made fakes should be labelled. The stricter rules for AI used in hiring or lending are still more than a year away.
